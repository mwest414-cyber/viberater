we've spent two posts on the why.

[why we built viberater](https://www.getviberater.co/blog/why-we-built-viberater) was the story. [the star rating is broken](https://www.getviberater.co/blog/the-star-rating-is-broken) was the argument. the short version of both: a star tells you if a place was good once, for somebody else, a while ago. it doesn't tell you what the room feels like tonight.

so this post is about the thing itself. when we say "vibe," we mean something specific. not a feeling you have to write a paragraph about. a structured read you can drop in seconds, and anyone else can understand at a glance.

a vibe is one energy level and a few tags. that's the whole grammar. here's every word in it.

## energy: how alive is the room

energy is the first thing you pick, and you pick exactly one. it's the single dial that runs from chill to electric.

- **chill:** calm, low-key, room to breathe and hear each other.
- **lively:** there's a pulse. people are out, things are moving.
- **buzzing:** full and warm, humming along, good night energy.
- **packed:** shoulder to shoulder, loud, going off.
- **electric:** the rare one. the night people text about tomorrow.

one place, one energy, right now. that's the headline of every vibe. it's also what colors the dot beside each venue, so you can read the energy at a glance before you pick a direction to walk.

## the tags: what kind of alive

energy tells you *how much*. the tags tell you *what kind*.

there are 31 of them, split across three plain questions: who's here, what does it feel like, what's the setup. you pick at least one and up to five, in any mix across the three. one tag is a quick read. five is a full picture. there's no wrong combination.

### crowd: who's in the room (8)

locals, tourists, young, mature, date night, solo friendly, groups, lgbtq

this is the "will i feel like i belong here" answer. a solo-friendly local spot and a groups-and-tourists spot are two completely different tuesdays, even at the same energy level.

### mood: what it feels like (9)

cozy, intimate, moody, flirty, gritty, friendly, dimly lit, main character, low-key

mood is the texture. it's the difference between a room that's buzzing-and-cozy and a room that's buzzing-and-gritty. same energy, opposite night.

### scene: what's the setup (14)

dive, bougie, dancy, live music, dj, outdoor seating, rooftop, dog friendly, laptop ok, dress up, sports, iykyk, loud, late night

scene is the practical stuff. can i bring the dog. is there a game on. do i need to dress up. is it a laptop-and-coffee afternoon or a dj-till-late kind of place. this is the bucket that answers "is this the right place for what i'm actually trying to do."

## why a handful of tags beats a single number

a number flattens everything. four-point-five stars can't tell you the room is intimate and candlelit, or electric with a little grit. it averages the texture right out.

the tags keep the texture. "chill · cozy · dimly lit" and "packed · dancy · loud" are both valid, useful, completely different reads, and each one is honest about what kind of night the place is offering. (we got into the deeper case for this in [the star rating is broken](https://www.getviberater.co/blog/the-star-rating-is-broken), if you want the full version.)

no stars. no numbers. just: what's it like in there, right now.

## the whole thing, in one read

put it together and a vibe looks like this:

> **buzzing** · date night · cozy · dimly lit

one energy level, a few tags, and you already know whether to walk in. that's the entire language. five energy levels, thirty-one tags, and a quick drop that tells a stranger everything they need to know before they get there.

know the vibe before you come inside.
